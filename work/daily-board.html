Why Daily Board

I already like using Apple Reminders and Calendar — I never wanted to replace them, just stop switching apps to see what my day actually looks like. For a while I kept a journal in one app and notes in another; neither survived, because neither lived where I already was. This first version deliberately starts small — Today, Journal, and Notes, entirely on-device — so there's something real to use, and real feedback to build the rest on.

See it in action

Real screens from the running app, not mockups.

Today — every reminder and calendar event on one timeline, because I got tired of switching apps just to see what my day actually looks like. Adding something new works the way it forms in my head: one sentence, what, when, where, not ten fields to tap through. Type "dentist tomorrow 10am" and it becomes a real Apple Reminder with that due date. Tap the circle to complete, swipe to reschedule. Journal — I've always found reflecting on the day genuinely valuable, but a journal in its own app is a journal I stop opening. Living right next to Today means I actually keep the habit. Write a few lines in the morning ("go for a walk and call my mom…") and the on-device AI proposes real to-dos from it — here "Call the dentist" and "Buy groceries" — no network call involved. Apple finally ships real on-device intelligence, so I wanted to actually use it. Notes — built to feel almost identical to Apple Notes, on purpose: thin and fast, not a note-taking platform in its own right. The only reason it exists at all is that Apple offers no public API into Notes the way it does for Reminders and Calendar, so anything living next to Today needs its own lightweight store. "Grocery list" here was never typed as a title — it's just the note's first line; set a custom one any time. Settings — for this first version: choose which calendar feeds Today, toggle morning and evening journal reminders, and export a local JSON backup with one tap. This is also where sync, automation and more AI will land as Daily Board grows from a personal daily driver into a real information hub — deliberately left minimal here until those are actually real, not just planned.

What's next

Daily Board isn't meant to be a second home screen — it's meant to keep growing into one real information center, not more apps. Here's what that means concretely:

Mail & messages

Which emails and messages still need a reply, surfaced right on Today instead of buried across three different inboxes.

Claude & AI sessions

Which AI sessions are waiting on my input, and where — so a running Claude session doesn't quietly stall just because I forgot to check back.

A personal second brain

Eventually pulling context from my own second brain instead of typing the same thing in twice — Daily Board becomes a window into it, not another place to feed.

Privacy, in full

Daily Board does not collect, store, or transmit any personal data to my or any third party's servers. This is the complete privacy policy, not a summary.

Reminders & Calendar
Displayed and edited in the daily board, stored on your device and synced only via your own iCloud account (Apple's technology, not mine). No second copy is ever kept.
Journal & Notes
Stored exclusively on-device (SwiftData). Deleted when you remove an entry, or when you uninstall the app.
On-device AI
Optional journal task suggestions run entirely locally via Apple's FoundationModels framework — no text is ever sent to a server, mine, Apple's, or anyone else's.
Backup
An optional local JSON backup (weekly or manual) lands in the app's own Documents folder, visible via the Files app — you decide if and where to move it. The app never uploads it automatically.
No sharing with third parties
No advertising, no analytics or tracking services, no data sharing, no in-app purchases, no cloud backend run by me.
